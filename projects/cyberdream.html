CyberDream: a handcrafted look, built for real-time VR

Modeling and texture painting for a VR party game during a 4-month internship. I matched a handmade, DIY art style and kept every asset within the technical limits of VR.

Role
3D Environment Artist: modeling, texturing & Unity implementation
Timeline
4-month internship, Jan – Apr 2022
Tools
Maya, Photoshop, Substance Painter, Unity
Team
Lead & two Environment Artists, Concept Artist, two Producers, Level Designers
01

The brief

Emerge Home hired CyberDream VR, a VR game studio, to make games for a virtual social space. The client wanted everything to look handcrafted, like a DIY project. As an environment artist, I modeled and painted assets that matched the concept artist's style guide, the rest of the art team's work and the engine's limits.

To get the handmade look, I thought about the materials someone would use to build each object, and the small imperfections they would leave behind.

02

Iterating on a volcano

Shape, color and contrast for the Sumo Slammers level.

Feedback · Silhouette

A more natural shape

My lead felt the first silhouette was too sharp. I studied real references, rounded the edges and broke the form into smaller rock chunks.

Feedback · Color & contrast

Readable at a glance

In-engine testing showed the volcano needed more contrast against the level. We moved to a dark gray rock, and I repainted the lava to follow the mountain's curves.

In-game

The final volcano in Sumo Slammers

In the finished level, the dark gray volcano stands out against the warm sky and orange ground, so players can read the scene at a glance while the lava adds movement in the background.

03

Working within limits

A handmade look that doesn't slow the game down.

The microwave shows how I painted rich detail while keeping file weight low.

Problem 01 · Texture memory

Solution: trim sheets

VR games have a tight texture budget, and too many separate textures slow the whole game down. I painted the microwave's details onto a single shared strip, called a trim sheet, so many surfaces could reuse one texture.

Problem 02 · Baking

Solution: a separate lightmap UV set

Trim sheets can break baked lighting, so I gave the model a second UV layout just for lighting and combined both in Unity.

04

What I took away

01

Work inside a style guide

I learned not to get attached to my own ideas and to match an agreed style across a whole team.

02

Test in context

The volcano looked fine on its own; the contrast problem only showed up in the real scene.

03

Constraints shape the design

Knowing the technical limits early, and preparing files for a clean handoff, made the art better, not worse.
